Chain-Driven Zoltar Kinetic Display

Onshape Assemblies Mechanical Prototyping Sprocket Spacing Plasma Cutting

1. Problem & Story

Create a chain-driven kinetic display coordinating motion between a rotating mystic ball, a fortune-dispensing mechanism, and interactive character components driven by a single motor setup.

2. Constraints & Requirements

  • Fixed Chain Path: Chain layout had to clear internal housing while engaging multiple sprockets simultaneously.
  • Tolerance Management: Interdependent linkages required tight alignment tolerances across 3D-printed, laser-cut, and metal components.

3. Design & Iteration Process

Subsystem functionality was isolated using physical proof-of-concept models for the fortune wheel, mystic ball, and ticket slider mechanisms before merging into the main assembly.

Engineering Challenges & Solutions

  • Sprocket Alignment & Friction: Uneven sprocket spacing caused chain binding. Calculated offset distances and incorporated precision spacers to maintain planar alignment.
  • Interdependent Clearance: Repositioned mounting points and modified internal clearancing to allow simultaneous rotation of the mystic ball and fortune wheel without collisions.

4. Results

Delivered a fully integrated kinetic display operating continuously on a single drive loop with minimal friction losses.

5. Lessons Learned & Reflections

Physical tolerances often deviate from ideal CAD constraints. Testing drive loads physically alongside assembly modeling allowed us to correct alignment issues early in the build cycle.
