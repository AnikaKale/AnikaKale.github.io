Project 03 Mechanism design

Arctic predator sculpture

A team-built sculpture that repeats an arctic predator-and-prey sequence. A motor moves a seal along a chain, and the seal’s interaction with a ramp triggers the shark’s jaw without a direct gear connection.

Focus
Passive actuation & contact geometry
Tools & methods
Onshape, plasma cutting, 3D printing
Context
Team project

Inside the project

01 The constraints 02 Design decisions 03 The outcome 04 What I learned

The constraints

  • Fixed drive geometry. Work around the existing motor, chain path, and mounting space.
  • Indirect actuation. Use the seal’s motion to move the jaw without a direct gear connection.
  • Contact and friction. Avoid ramp shapes and contact points that cause the mechanism to bind.

Design decisions

Plan the sequence of motion

Whiteboard sketches established the motion paths before physical mockups explored how force would transfer between the seal, ramp, and shark jaw.

Refine the ramp geometry

Early flat and curved ramp profiles caused binding. The contact geometry was revised, and the surfaces were hand-sanded to improve the engagement between the moving parts.

Connect the seal to the drive

A custom steel L-bracket was designed and plasma cut because the available hardware did not provide the needed connection between the seal and chain.

The outcome

The completed sculpture ran a repeating motor-driven sequence in which the chain-mounted seal triggered the shark’s jaw. The final mechanism combined the custom bracket, ramp, and jaw interaction.

What I learned

This project made me pay closer attention to the contact between moving parts. I would test the ramp and jaw together before integrating them with the rest of the sculpture, so changes to the geometry are easier to make.
