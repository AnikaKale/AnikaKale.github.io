Project 05 Electrical integration

Automotive ambient lighting

A personal project to integrate an interior lighting kit into my car without its original cigarette-lighter connection. The work involved adapting the power leads, connecting through a fuse tap, adding a chassis ground, and routing the wiring behind the interior panels.

Focus
Power connections & installation
Tools & methods
Fuse tap, soldering, heat shrink
Context
Personal project

Inside the project

01 The constraints 02 Design decisions 03 The outcome 04 What I learned

The constraints

  • Switched operation. Have the lights operate with the ignition rather than remain on while parked.
  • Connection and routing. Make room for the added wiring and protect the connections behind the dash panels.
  • Factory wiring. Use a fuse tap and an existing chassis grounding point to avoid cutting the vehicle’s original harness.

Design decisions

Adapt the kit’s connections

The original plug connection was replaced with connections through a fuse tap and an existing chassis grounding point. A switched circuit was selected so the lights would operate with the ignition.

Rework a connection that did not hold

The fuse tap’s original crimp connector did not secure the small power wire as intended. The joint was reworked with solder and covered with heat-shrink tubing.

Complete the installation

The negative lead was attached at a bare-metal chassis grounding point. Wiring was routed behind the interior panels to keep the connections out of the visible cabin area.

The outcome

The installed lights turned on and off with the ignition. The added wiring and connections were positioned behind the interior panels for a cleaner installation.

What I learned

This project gave me practical experience with switched power, chassis grounding, and electrical connections. The connector problem showed me why I need to check how a joint holds together instead of judging it only by its appearance.
