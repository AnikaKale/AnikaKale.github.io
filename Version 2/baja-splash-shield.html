Project 01 Designing with Constraints

Baja SAE splash shield

A custom splash shield designed to fit between the fuel tank and engine of our existing Baja SAE car to protect the engine from stray fuel and keep the driver safe.

Focus
Packaging, runoff geometry & mounting hardware
Tools & methods
CAD modeling, sheet fabrication, tabs & jigs
Context
Baja SAE

Inside the project

01 Constraints 02 Design iterations 03 Fabrication & troubleshooting 04 Takeaways

Constraints

  • Existing geometry: Needed to fit in between the fuel tank and engine while being able to be assembled considering the existing tubular frame.
  • Runoff path: All fuel must be directed off the sides or rear of the vehicle, must be fully constructed of metal at least 0.02 in thick
  • Engine Clearance: Must have 13 mm clearance to the exhaust and be mounted below structural member for the fuel tank

Design Iterations: Learning to Design for Manufacturing

My design went through three major revisions as I learned to design for manufacturing

Version 1

I modeled a five piece splash guard.

In my first design review, a teammate asked, "Why five parts?" and I didn’t have a good answer. They mentioned that more pieces means more unnecessary effort both to fab the parts and then also to put it together.

Version 2

I went back to the drawing board and reduced the design to three pieces. During the next review, I was asked if I was sure fuel wouldn’t come in contact with the engine. A senior engineer on the team pointed out how fuel directed off the back would still hit the engine as the frame of our car jutted out farther than I had expected.

Version 3

My final version looked completely different from the first two. It was a single piece designed specifically for a sheet metal brake, that directed all fuel fully off the sides of the car where there was a wide clearance between the sides of the splash guard and the engine.

Fabrication & Hands-On Troubleshooting

Before I cut the splash guard, I got to manufacture the firewall to learn how to use the CNC plasma cutter.

Plasma Cutting Battles

Cutting the firewall took three attempts and eight total hours across three days. We lost the material on the first try, used the wrong material on the second, and finally succeeded on the third, but only after fighting with unconstrained legacy CAD files that confused the machine’s software.

Sheet Metal Bending

Bending the final splash guard came with its own issues. My part had tight geometry that I hadn't considered in CAD, so it took a few tries, and some advice from shop staff, to get right.

Tabs and Jigs

The hardest part of the entire project was attaching the guard. The mounting tabs all had to sit on angled chassis tubes. I learned how to create custom construction planes in Onshape to model them, then spent a couple of hours with my Project Manager figuring out how to construct the oddly fit tabs and their physical jigs to get everything ready for our welder.

Takeaways

This project opened my world up to CAD and taught me some important lessons early on. I realized you can't only draw something that fits digitally, but also design based on what the machines can actually do in the shop and compare the ease of fabrication to the benefits a more challenging design brings.

But, most importantly, I learned how to recognize when I was stuck and then ask the right people for help so I could keep moving.
