Project 01 Vehicle integration

Baja SAE splash shield

A custom splash shield designed to fit between the fuel tank and engine of a Baja SAE car. The project combined CAD modeling, panel fabrication, and mounting features within the existing chassis.

Focus
Packaging, runoff geometry & mounting
Tools & methods
CAD modeling, sheet fabrication, tabs & jigs
Context
Baja SAE

Inside the project

01 The constraints 02 Design decisions 03 The outcome 04 What I learned

The constraints

  • Existing geometry. Fit between the fuel tank, engine, and tubular frame without obstructing surrounding components.
  • Runoff path. Shape the panels to direct spills toward the sides or rear of the vehicle.
  • Assembly access. Make the panel geometry and mounting features practical to fabricate and assemble.

Design decisions

Work around the existing chassis

The design developed into an assembly of roughly 15–20 parts, combining HDPE panels and aluminum mounting brackets. Several CAD revisions addressed the limited space between the fuel tank, engine, and tubular frame. Design reviews with teammates helped refine the panel shapes and clearances.

Make the parts easier to assemble

Custom jigs and self-locating tabs were developed to help position the parts during bending and assembly. These features made alignment part of the design rather than something left entirely to the final assembly.

The outcome

The completed shield was mounted in the engine bay. Its panel shapes were intended to guide spills toward the sides and rear of the vehicle while fitting around the surrounding components.

What I learned

This project taught me to bring clearance questions into design reviews early. It also showed me how tabs and jigs can make the transition from a CAD model to a physical assembly easier.
