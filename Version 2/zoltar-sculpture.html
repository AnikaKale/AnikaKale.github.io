Project 02 Mechanism design

Chain-driven Zoltar

A team-built kinetic display combining a rotating mystic ball, a fortune wheel, and a ticket mechanism. A single motor and chain drive powered the system, making alignment and clearance between the moving assemblies central to the design.

Focus
Sprocket alignment & subsystem integration
Tools & methods
Onshape, physical prototypes
Context
Team project

Inside the project

01 The constraints 02 Design decisions 03 The outcome 04 What I learned

The constraints

  • Shared drive. Coordinate the moving mechanisms around one motor and chain path.
  • Chain clearance. Engage the sprockets while keeping the chain clear of the display housing.
  • Part alignment. Align 3D-printed, laser-cut, and metal components while leaving room for the mechanisms to move.

Design decisions

Prototype the mechanisms separately

Physical prototypes explored the fortune wheel, mystic ball, and ticket slider before they were integrated into the display. This made it possible to work through the individual motions before addressing the complete assembly.

Correct sprocket spacing

Uneven sprocket spacing caused the chain to bind. Offsets and spacers were adjusted to bring the sprockets into the same plane.

Adjust the internal layout

Mounting points and internal clearances changed to make room for the mystic ball and fortune wheel to rotate together without colliding.

The outcome

The separate mechanisms were integrated into a completed display operating from one drive loop. The final assembly brought the ball, fortune wheel, and ticket mechanism into a shared system.

What I learned

This build showed me how small alignment differences can affect an entire chain-driven system. Testing the physical mechanisms alongside the CAD model helped reveal problems that were difficult to judge from the model alone.
