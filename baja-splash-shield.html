BAJA SAE Engine Splash Shield

CAD Modeling Plasma Cutting Jigs & Fixturing Design for Manufacturing

1. Problem & Story

Design and fabricate a protective splash shield to serve as a barrier between the fuel tank and the engine, strictly conforming to the existing, highly constrained geometry of the Baja SAE chassis.

2. Constraints & Requirements

  • Fluid Evacuation: The panel geometry must guarantee zero fuel pooling under any operational condition.
  • Directed Runoff: All deflected fuel must be routed completely off the vehicle, exiting exclusively out the sides or the rear.
  • Spatial Clearances: The shield had to navigate tight clearances between engine bay components and the existing tubular spaceframe.

3. Design & Iteration Process

The design evolved into a 15–20 part assembly combining custom profiled HDPE panels and laser-cut aluminum mounting brackets. Because the spatial constraints were unforgiving, I brought the CAD through multiple design reviews, learning how to effectively ask for technical help and critique from upperclassmen to refine the clearances.

Engineering Challenges & Execution

  • Iterative CAD: Navigated the steep learning curve of CAD basics by modeling the vehicle constraints first, testing fluid runoff angles, and adjusting panel slopes to prevent pooling.
  • Fabrication & Tooling: Utilized plasma cutting for the metal components. To ensure accurate bends and precise assembly alignment, I designed 3D modeled jigs and integrated self-locating tabs directly into the sheet parts.

4. Results

The completed splash shield integrated securely into the car's engine bay, successfully routing potential spills off the rear and sides of the vehicle without interfering with surrounding mechanical systems.

5. What I Learned

This project was a crash course in designing within rigid physical constraints and mastering foundational CAD skills. On the manufacturing side, it taught me practical fabrication techniques, specifically plasma cutting and designing custom tabs and jigs to simplify physical assembly. Most importantly, navigating formal design reviews showed me the immense value of communicating roadblocks early and knowing how to ask the team for help.
