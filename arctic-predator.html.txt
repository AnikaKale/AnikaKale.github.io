        <h2>Project Goal</h2>
        <p>Design a motor-driven kinetic sculpture that communicates a looping arctic predator-prey narrative through consistent, repeatable motion across multiple interacting mechanisms without chain interference.</p>

        <div class="challenge-box">
            <h3>Engineering Challenges & Solutions</h3>
            <ul>
                <li><strong>Indirect Motion Transfer:</strong> Countered chain interference risks by utilizing a custom seal and ramp system to passively actuate the shark head mechanism.</li>
                <li><strong>Ramp Geometry & Jamming:</strong> Initial flat and curved ramp variations caused motor stalling. Iterated the geometry and applied manual sanding/refinement to ensure smooth fit and motion.</li>
                <li><strong>Fabrication Customization:</strong> Needed a secure chain connection when no standard part fit. Designed and plasma cut a custom steel L-bracket, managing file prep and cutting workflows.</li>
            </ul>
        </div>

        <h2>Reflection</h2>
        <p>This project reinforced the importance of subsystem prototyping. Early physical interaction tests exposed critical jamming points before full assembly. In future iterations, designing subsystems to test more independently would minimize delays caused by changing layout constraints.</p>
