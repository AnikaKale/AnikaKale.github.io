Kinetic Arctic Predator Chain Sculpture

Onshape Multi-part Assemblies Plasma Cutting 3D Printing Interdependent Systems

1. Problem & Story

Design a motor-driven kinetic sculpture that communicates a looping arctic predator-prey narrative through consistent, repeatable motion across multiple interacting mechanical subsystems without motor stalling or chain interference.

2. Constraints & Requirements

  • Geometry & Spacing: Sourced motor and chain drive imposed fixed spatial constraints for mechanical mounting.
  • Kinematic Coupling: Motion from the main seal drive had to passively actuate the shark bite without direct gear linkage.
  • Load Limits: Minimize motor friction to prevent power supply overloading and drive binding.

3. Design & Iteration Process

Early whiteboard planning established motion paths before testing force transfer on physical mockups.

Engineering Challenges & Solutions

  • Indirect Motion Transfer: Countered chain interference by designing a seal and ramp mechanism to passively actuate the shark jaw.
  • Ramp Geometry & Jamming: Initial flat and curved ramps caused binding. Iterated profile geometry and hand-sanded contact surfaces to achieve smooth engagement.
  • Custom Bracketry: Standard hardware could not adapt the seal to the chain drive; designed and plasma cut a custom steel L-bracket.

4. Results

Achieved a continuous motor-driven loop where the chain-driven seal triggers the shark jaw interaction reliably with zero binding.

5. Lessons Learned & Reflections

This project highlighted the importance of modular subsystem isolation. In future designs, creating test rigs for independent subsystems prior to final integration will reduce delays caused by changing geometry constraints.
